"Storage full" is the notification that stops everything: updates refuse to install, the camera fails to save a photo, and WhatsApp messages stop downloading. It is also one of the most fixable problems on an Android phone, because most storage is consumed by things you never deliberately saved - caches, duplicates, forgotten downloads, and media sitting in two places at once.

This guide walks through 12 steps in order of how much space they typically return. The first five are safe for anyone and often free 10 to 20 GB. Later steps involve trade-offs, and each one explains the trade-off so you can decide rather than guess. Nothing here requires a cleaner app, rooting, or connecting to a computer - though we mention where a computer helps.

You will need your phone, about 30 minutes, and a willingness to check one number before and after: open **Settings → Storage** and write down the free space now. By the end you will see exactly how much you reclaimed.

## Step 1: Read the Storage Breakdown

Before deleting anything, understand where the space actually went. **Settings → Storage** shows categories - Apps, Images, Videos, Audio, Documents, System - with a bar chart of what each occupies.

Tap into each category. Android's breakdown frequently surprises people: "Apps" can dwarf every photo you have ever taken, and "System" often claims 15 to 30 GB because it holds the OS, recovery partition and update files you cannot remove.

The categories that usually hide the real culprits are **Apps** (sorted by size reveals the monsters), **Documents and other files** (old PDFs, downloads), and whatever messaging apps report internally. Also check **Downloads** from the Files app - it is where browser downloads accumulate unnoticed for years.

## Step 2: Clear App Caches (Safe, Fast, No Data Loss)

Apps cache images, video thumbnails and temporary data to load faster. Over months this reaches gigabytes, and clearing it costs you nothing permanent.

Go to **Settings → Apps → See all apps**, tap an app, then **Storage and cache → Clear cache**. Do this for the heavy hitters first: social media apps, browsers, streaming apps and games. Repeat for the top five or ten apps in the list.

**Clear cache ≠ clearing data.** Cache is disposable. "Clear storage" or "Clear data" resets the app entirely - logins gone, settings gone, drafts gone. Only use that for apps where signing in again is painless, and never for your messaging apps before a chat transfer (see our [WhatsApp transfer guide](/posts/how-to-transfer-whatsapp-chats-to-a-new-phone/) for why order matters).

On many phones you can manage this centrally: **Settings → Storage → Cached data** (or "Temporary files") offers a one-tap clear for all apps at once.

## Step 3: Free Up Photos Properly

Photos and videos are usually the largest category, and the trickiest to clean because nobody wants to delete memories.

1. **Back up first.** Open Google Photos, confirm your backup is complete (avatar → Back up), and wait for it to finish. If you do not use Google Photos, copy everything to a computer before deleting anything - our [iPhone backup guide](/posts/how-to-back-up-your-iphone/) explains the principles that apply across platforms.
2. **Remove local copies with the built-in tool.** In Google Photos, tap Profile → **Free up space**. This deletes the phone copies of pictures that are safely backed up, without touching the cloud library. It is the correct way to reclaim photo storage.
3. **Empty the trash.** Deleted photos sit in the trash for 30 or 60 days, still occupying space. Google Photos → Library → Trash → **Empty trash**. Check your gallery app's trash too; most gallery apps keep a separate one.
4. **Find duplicates and screenshots.** Google Photos' search for "screenshots" and "duplicates" surfaces the easy wins. Screenshots accumulate fastest and are rarely needed beyond a week.

## Step 4: Audit Video Files

Video files are 10 to 50 times heavier than photos. Look for: screen recordings, slow-motion clips shot by accident, videos already shared to social platforms, and long clips from events you have already edited.

Sort your gallery or Files app by size, descending, and delete the top offenders you no longer need. If you record in 4K and rarely watch in 4K, switch the camera to 1080p (**Camera settings → Video quality**) - it halves storage use going forward and nobody can tell the difference on a phone screen.

## Step 5: Manage Downloads and Documents

Open the **Files by Google** app (or your file manager) and check **Downloads**. Typical finds: installers for apps you already installed, duplicate PDFs, images saved from messaging apps, and old work documents.

Sort by size and delete ruthlessly for anything you have already used. For documents you might need later but not on the phone, move them to Google Drive, Dropbox or a computer. Cloud storage costs nothing at entry level and turns your phone back into a phone rather than an archive.

Files by Google doubles as a cleaner: its **Clean** tab suggests deletable junk and duplicate files, and unlike third-party cleaner ads it does not push you toward installing more apps.

## Step 6: Tame Messaging Apps

WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger and Signal all save received media automatically. A busy family group can add gigabytes a month without you noticing.

In WhatsApp: **Settings → Storage and data → Media auto-download** - set photos and videos to download only when you choose (or Wi-Fi only, and then delete what you do not keep). Then open **Manage storage** inside the same menu: it ranks chats by size and lets you bulk-delete large files while keeping the conversation.

In Telegram: Settings → Data and Storage → Storage path, and use the "Auto-remove media from device" options. Telegram's cloud keeps your messages even when local copies are gone.

Do this for your top three messaging apps and you will typically recover several gigabytes.

## Step 7: Remove Apps You Do Not Use

**Settings → Apps → See all apps**, then sort by size (or use Files → Apps → App size). Two years of installing means two years of accumulation: games finished, utilities replaced, trial apps forgotten.

Uninstall what you have not opened in a month. Where an app came pre-installed as bloatware and cannot be uninstalled, use **Disable** instead - it stops the app from running or updating and frees the space it would otherwise keep occupying.

Keep anything you use seasonally (airline apps, tax software) but consider re-downloading it from the Play Store when needed; your purchase and download history makes reinstallation trivial.

## Step 8: Offload or Move Apps to an SD Card

If your phone has an SD card slot, you can move compatible apps and media to it. In **Settings → Apps → [App] → Storage**, choose **Move to SD card** when the option appears (availability varies by app and manufacturer).

For photos, set your camera to save directly to the SD card in camera settings. Test the card after switching - a cheap card that corrupts photos is worse than full internal storage.

Phones without SD slots can still offload: **Settings → Apps → [App] → Archive** (Android's app archiving keeps data while removing the app body) or use Google's **Saved by Android** storage manager on supported devices.

## Step 9: Stream Instead of Store

Music and podcast apps cache entire libraries. In Spotify: **Settings → Storage → Remove download** for playlists you have finished, and set an automatic storage limit. In YouTube Music or similar, remove offline downloads for playlists you no longer listen to.

The same applies to offline maps (Google Maps → Settings → Offline maps → manage or delete downloaded regions), offline documents in reading apps, and streaming apps that pre-download episodes ("Download over Wi-Fi only" is the setting you want).

## Step 10: Check What "System" Is Hiding

If Settings reports a large System or "Other" figure, some of it is reclaimable:

- **Old update files:** Files → Downloads may contain downloaded OTA zips from manufacturer apps; delete them.
- **Bloatware updates:** some phones accumulate multiple versions of system app updates in Settings → Apps → Show system.
- **Recovery and cache partitions** are genuinely fixed - do not chase them.

If System is eating more than 30 percent of a large phone and performance suffers as well as space, the nuclear option is a factory reset with a clean restore (Step 12) - it is the only way to return system storage to factory distribution.

## Step 11: Prevent the Creep

Storage fills again unless you build habits. Three habits keep it drained:

1. **Monthly two-minute cleanup:** empty the trash in your gallery, clear caches, check Downloads. Add it to the same calendar reminder as your [weekly planning session](/posts/how-to-plan-your-week/).
2. **Auto-delete backed-up photos:** in Google Photos settings, enable auto-delete of backed-up screenshots after 30 or 90 days.
3. **Cap messaging downloads:** set media auto-download to Wi-Fi only, as in Step 6, so groups stop filling your phone in the background.

## Step 12: Factory Reset as a Last Resort

When a phone is still tight after all of the above - or when it is slow as well as full - a factory reset gives you a fresh start. This deletes **everything**, so:

1. Confirm your Google account backup is complete (Settings → Google → Backup).
2. Export your 2FA codes and authenticator apps to a safe place first - losing these can lock you out of other accounts.
3. Save your eSIM details and note your apps (Google Play Store → Library shows installed apps).
4. Set a reminder for the morning after: restore method, Google account, and messaging apps are the first three things you will want.

Then: **Settings → System → Reset options → Erase all data (factory reset)**. Set the phone up from scratch rather than restoring a full backup when you can - that is what makes it a fresh start instead of a restoration of the problem.

## Troubleshooting Storage Mysteries

**"Other" or "System" storage keeps growing.** Mostly caches and temporary files; clearing app caches (Step 2) and rebooting into recovery to wipe the cache partition reduces it. Persistent growth after that often points to a misbehaving app - check which app last updated when the growth started.

**Storage full but no big files found.** Look at individual app storage: **Settings → Apps → [App] → Storage**. Games, navigation apps and video editors routinely hold 5-20 GB internally in "data", not media.

**SD card not recognized.** Re-seat the card, format it as portable storage (Settings → Storage → SD card → Format), and remember that portable cards cannot host some protected apps - use it for media instead.

**Phone slow after clearing space.** The first launch after clearing caches is slower while apps rebuild them. Give it a day; if slowness persists, it is unrelated to storage - see our [slow laptop fixes](/posts/how-to-speed-up-a-slow-laptop/) for the same diagnostic logic applied to computers.

## When to Get Help

A phone whose storage keeps filling despite cleaning may have a failing storage chip - symptoms include apps crashing randomly, the camera refusing to save, and free space jumping up and down. Back up immediately and visit the manufacturer's service centre; storage chips are not user-replaceable.

Otherwise, the manual route wins every time: read the breakdown, clear caches, back up and release photos, fix messaging auto-downloads, prune downloads and apps. Most phones come out of this process with 20 to 40 GB reclaimed and no cleaner app in sight.
