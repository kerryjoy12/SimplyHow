Your WhatsApp history is a diary: years of messages, the photos your family sends, work instructions you still need, and group chats you cannot leave. Losing it during a phone upgrade is one of the most common migration regrets - and entirely avoidable, as long as you know which of the three routes applies to you and run it in the right order.

WhatsApp does not sync chats across devices the way email does. Your history lives on **one primary phone** at a time (with a separate linked-computer experience), so moving it means a deliberate migration. There are three official paths:

1. **Android → Android** using a Google Drive backup.
2. **iPhone → iPhone** using an iCloud backup.
3. **Cross-platform (Android ↔ iPhone)** using the official Move to iOS app or a direct cable connection during setup.

This guide covers all three step by step, then shows you how to verify every last chat arrived and what to do when a transfer fails. The same principles apply whether you are upgrading every two years or switching ecosystems after a decade.

## Before You Start: The Five-Point Checklist

Skipping preparation causes almost every failed transfer. Spend ten minutes here first.

1. **Use the same phone number.** Your WhatsApp account is tied to your number. Verify the same number on the new phone or you get a fresh account with no history.
2. **Charge both phones to at least 50 percent** (ideally plug them in). Transfers take longer than people expect and a mid-transfer power-off can corrupt the process.
3. **Free up space on the new phone.** WhatsApp media adds up; if the new device has less free space than your chat history occupies, the restore will abort. Check under Settings → Storage in WhatsApp to see your real footprint.
4. **Update WhatsApp on both devices** to the latest version from the Play Store or App Store. Migrations work best when both sides speak the same version.
5. **Make a fresh backup on the old phone.** Open WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat backup and tap **Back Up**. A backup from last week is missing this week's messages - and people always notice the missing week.

One more piece of mental housekeeping: understand that media you have deleted from your phone may still exist in your backup, and vice versa. If there are photos you must keep, export them first - our [iPhone backup guide](/posts/how-to-back-up-your-iphone/) explains the difference between synced and backed-up data.

## Method 1: Android to Android (Google Drive)

This is the easiest migration when both phones run Android.

### Step 1: Confirm the backup account

On the old phone, open WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat backup. Note the **Google Account** shown under Google Account settings - you will need that exact account on the new phone. If the backup says "Never", tap **Back Up** and wait for it to finish; check that the backup timestamp updates.

### Step 2: Install WhatsApp on the new phone

Power on the new Android phone, complete initial setup, install WhatsApp from the Play Store, and open it. Verify your phone number by SMS as prompted.

### Step 3: Restore the chat history

When the **Restore your chats** screen appears, tap **Restore** and choose the same Google account as the old phone. WhatsApp downloads your message database first, then media in the background. Keep the app open and the phone on Wi-Fi until the process finishes.

If you do not see the restore prompt, WhatsApp may have found no backup for that number-account combination: reinstall (or clear the app's data) and try again with the correct Google account.

### Step 4: Let media finish downloading

Messages appear first; photos and videos fill in progressively. Leave the phone on Wi-Fi and plugged in until Settings → Storage shows the size you expect. If media stays missing after an hour, open Settings → Chats → Chat backup and re-run it - sometimes a second pass picks up stragglers.

## Method 2: iPhone to iPhone (iCloud)

### Step 1: Back up the old iPhone

Open WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat backup on the old iPhone. If you have not enabled iCloud for WhatsApp, the screen will prompt you to **Back Up to iCloud**; make sure the backup completes and the timestamp shows today. Insufficient iCloud storage is the number one failure here - see our [iCloud backup guide](/posts/how-to-back-up-your-iphone/) for how to clear space.

### Step 2: Prepare the new iPhone

If you are setting up a brand-new iPhone, complete initial setup until you reach the Apps & Data screen. If you are reusing an iPhone, erase it first: Settings → General → Transfer or Reset iPhone → Erase All Content and Settings - **only after** you are certain the old phone's backup is complete.

### Step 3: Restore during setup

When WhatsApp installs on the new iPhone (either via restored apps during setup or a fresh App Store install), open it, verify your number, and choose **Restore Chat History** when prompted. WhatsApp detects the iCloud backup associated with your Apple ID and phone number.

Keep the phone on Wi-Fi and open until the progress bar completes. Media downloads in the background afterwards.

## Method 3: Android to iPhone (and Back) - The Official Path

Cross-platform transfers were historically impossible; WhatsApp now supports them directly.

### Android → iPhone via Move to iOS

1. On the **old Android phone**, install **Move to iOS** from the Play Store and open it.
2. Start setting up the **new iPhone** until you reach **Apps & Data**, then tap **Move Data from Android**. (If the iPhone is already set up, you can still use Move to iOS to transfer selected data, but chat migration is most reliable during initial setup.)
3. On the Android phone, tap **Continue**, agree to permissions, and enter the one-time code shown on the iPhone.
4. When the content list appears, enable **WhatsApp** among the selected data and tap **Continue**. The iPhone displays "Preparing..." while WhatsApp packages your history.
5. Follow the remaining prompts to finish iPhone setup, then install WhatsApp from the App Store, verify your number, and tap **Import Chat History** when offered.

Keep both phones plugged in and next to each other; do not switch apps or lock the Android phone during the transfer.

### Android → iPhone via cable

For large histories, a wired connection is more stable than Wi-Fi. During iPhone setup choose **Move Data from Android**, connect both phones with a USB-C to USB-C (or USB-C to Lightning) cable, unlock the Android phone and approve the connection, then select WhatsApp in the data list as above.

### iPhone → Android

Use the **Move to Android** app on your Android phone (or the built-in cable transfer during Android setup), select WhatsApp in the transfer list, and follow the prompts. After the transfer completes, install WhatsApp on Android, verify your number and choose to import the history.

**Important:** the iPhone copy of your account becomes inactive once WhatsApp is verified on the Android phone - that is normal, since only one primary device holds your account at a time.

## Verifying the Transfer

Do not power off or sell the old phone yet. Open the new phone's WhatsApp and check:

1. **The chat list** - every conversation should be present, in the same order, with unread counts intact.
2. **Spot-check old conversations** - scroll to a thread from a year ago and confirm messages load.
3. **Media** - open a few photo-heavy chats; images and videos should appear once background downloads finish.
4. **Groups** - confirm group names, participants and your admin status.
5. **Settings** - starred messages, muted chats, blocked numbers and your profile photo should all carry over.
6. **Starred messages and documents** - open Starred chats and a couple of document-heavy threads.

Only when everything checks out should you remove WhatsApp from the old phone. If the old phone will be traded in, sign out of WhatsApp first (Settings → Account → Log out) as an extra precaution, then perform a full factory reset of the device so none of your data remains - our [smartphone storage guide](/posts/how-to-free-up-space-on-your-android-phone/) covers safe device clean-up steps.

## Troubleshooting Failed Transfers

**"Restore" button greyed out or no backup found.** The backup is attached to a different Google account or Apple ID, or the phone number differs. Re-check both, and confirm the backup timestamp on the old phone.

**Transfer starts then stalls at a percentage.** Network instability, most often. Move both phones near the router, disable any VPN, keep screens awake (adjust auto-lock to Never temporarily), and retry. Cable methods almost always succeed where Wi-Fi fails.

**Not enough storage on the new phone.** Free space by removing demo content, or transfer media separately first (Google Photos or a computer), then restore messages without media and re-download media later.

**Old chats missing after restore.** The backup predates those messages. Check whether your archived chats are included (Archived threads restore too - look in the Archived section), and whether you are viewing the correct WhatsApp account if you have dual-SIM or two numbers.

**Media won't download after a successful text restore.** Stay on Wi-Fi, open Settings → Storage and media, confirm auto-download settings, and check that the new phone has enough free space. Media downloaded from iCloud or Google Drive counts against that storage.

**Groups missing.** Reinstall and re-verify - groups re-attach automatically to your number, and they sometimes appear only after the first sync completes. If a group truly does not return, ask an admin to re-add you; the membership is recoverable even when the history is not.

## Keeping Your Chats Safe Going Forward

After the migration, set up a routine so this is the last stressful transfer:

- **Automatic cloud backups:** Settings → Chats → Chat backup → set the schedule to Daily, choose Wi-Fi-only, and include video only if you have the storage.
- **Monthly verification:** open the backup screen and confirm the timestamp - the same discipline we recommend in the [iPhone backup routine](/posts/how-to-back-up-your-iphone/).
- **Export critical threads:** for contracts, receipts or family history, use More → Export chat (without media is tiny; with media is large) and store the export somewhere durable.
- **Watch the media creep:** WhatsApp media is often the largest single item on a phone. Our [digital declutter guide](/posts/how-to-declutter-your-digital-life/) has a routine for trimming it without losing what matters.

## When Something Is Truly Lost

If the old phone is broken, wiped, or already reset without a backup, the chances of recovery are slim - WhatsApp does not keep a cloud copy of your messages on its servers for you to pull. In that situation, avoid "recovery" apps that promise the impossible and charge upfront; a legitimate data-recovery lab can sometimes pull data from a physically damaged phone, but only while the storage chip still works. Act fast, keep the device powered off, and get quotes before anyone attempts a repair that may overwrite data.

## The Bottom Line

Transferring WhatsApp is a five-minute job when you do it in the right order: back up first, install and verify with the same number on the new phone, restore during setup, and verify before touching the old device. Android to Android uses Google Drive, iPhone to iPhone uses iCloud, and cross-platform moves use Move to iOS or a cable - all official, all free. Spend the ten minutes on preparation and your years of conversations will arrive intact.
